# Working principles

The criteria the author applies when choosing between methods, sources and orders of work. Every skill in this repository follows them; a skill step that conflicts with a principle is wrong. The list grows as new criteria surface in practice; each entry carries the reason it exists.

## Quality before speed

1. Accuracy over speed. Think the answer through before giving it; a slower correct answer beats a fast plausible one. A quick move from topic to design to draft was set aside and redone.
2. Depth before commitment. About one hundred full-text papers are read before the research design is frozen; "no paper does X" is not a gap statement until the nearest papers and the authors who name the gap are counted.
3. Differentiation on two axes. The study must differ from the literature in its framing and in its method, and the difference must matter to the market or institution it describes, not only to the literature.

## Evidence

4. Only SCI, SCIE or SSCI journal articles count as literature. Mega open-access venues (MDPI, Frontiers, Heliyon and similar) are not treated as papers. Preprints and reports may inform context but are never cited as evidence.
5. Cite only what was obtained and read in full. Papers not obtained are not cited; a number quoted from a non-published version carries a verify flag until the published version is checked; journals with unconfirmed indexing carry a verify flag until the Master Journal List is checked.
6. Every number in a document traces to a results file or a reading record; tables, figures and reference lists are generated by code from those files, never typed by hand.
7. Data must actually be downloadable. A topic whose core data is private or unconfirmed is ranked down; old data are not reused if the years do not match.

## Framing and communication

8. Academic contribution first, institutional relevance last, when ranking topics.
9. Every deliverable opens with a plain-language paragraph: what the paper asks, what it is not, how it links to the author's field or brief. The object of the paper (cost, strategy, market) comes before the technique (forecasting, model) in titles.
10. Explain simply. The author may be new to the paper type; analogies and short options with a recommended choice are preferred to long surveys.
11. Decisions are the author's. Open choices are listed with options and a recommendation; "all as recommended" is an acceptable answer; results documents end with the decisions the author must make before writing.

## Process discipline

12. Stages are separate and each ends with a confirmed deliverable: ideation, literature review, data collection, data analysis, writing, review. Later stages do not silently redo earlier ones.
13. Earlier rounds are kept, marked superseded, never deleted. A new round reproduces the previous base case before anything new is reported.
14. Writing follows published key papers from the target journal at paragraph and sentence level; only approved paragraphs are assembled.
15. Requests to the author are batched. Papers that cannot be obtained after reasonable attempts are skipped; only must-have items go to the author, in one list, with DOI, title and reason. Permission prompts, logins and robot checks are the author's to handle and are never bypassed.
16. Prose is checked against code and data, not against prose. A claim about direction, mechanism or an institutional rule is verified against the model code, the result components and the reading record; agreement between two sentences of the same draft proves nothing. The review stage found a bias-direction explanation that was internally consistent and opposite to the code.
17. Delegated findings are hypotheses. Whatever an agent or colleague reports is re-verified before it is acted on, and a rejected proposal is recorded with the reason, because a confident wrong fix costs more than the error it replaces.
18. Standing approval is explicit and bounded. "Proceed with every recommendation until a stated time" is followed literally: every decision taken that way is logged as "taken as recommended" in the ledger and memory so the author can reverse it, and anything that needs the author's own hand is still asked in one line.
19. Lessons are recorded. Corrections the author gives become a line in the relevant skill, with the reason, so the next paper starts from them.
20. The target journal is a cascade, not a single fit. Structure and flow (the introduction's logic, the separation of methods, results and discussion, table and figure standards) follow the best journal in the field; details that belong to one journal only (a word limit, highlights, a graphical abstract) are met at submission and never by cutting approved content. If the first journal rejects, the reviews are addressed and the manuscript moves to the next journal of the same family, pre-chosen with the first. Top journals accept roughly one paper in five, so a manuscript shaped for one venue alone costs a second round of work. A cascade whose rungs are all top-tier is not a cascade: the first rung may be one ambitious attempt, each rung below is a clear step down, and the last is a journal where acceptance is realistic. Each rung's fit is argued from the journals in the paper's own reference list, its level and indexing are read from the journal's official metrics page rather than from third-party sites, and the paper's weaknesses as an editor would see them (a plain method, an assumed parameter, one country) are stated so that the author knows which rung is the stretch and which is the floor. The author's words, after the cascade had already been set once: "the journals we are targeting are too high, I am worried".

21. Small decisions are taken, not asked. When a primary source cannot be obtained after two attempts and the sentence it supports does not change a result (a price level, a background fact), the fallback is chosen as recommended (a transparent secondary source such as a trade-press report of the document, or dropping the sentence), logged in the ledger as "primary unavailable, cited X instead", and reported to the author as an outcome. Questions to the author are reserved for what only the author can do: account-gated downloads, author and affiliation details, and the submission itself. The author's words when asked to choose between fetching a report and accepting the press citation: "this much you can do yourself".
22. The author reads the whole manuscript before submission. Paragraph-level approval during writing is followed by review rounds applied "as recommended", so by submission the text contains sentences the author has never seen. Before the package is uploaded, the author reads the manuscript once from start to end, helped by a plain-language reading guide in the author's language (per section: what it claims, the key numbers, the points the author must agree with, what changed since approval) and, when useful, a faithful translation of the whole manuscript. Corrections go back into the source files and the package is rebuilt; the author's own name is on the paper and the author answers the referees.
