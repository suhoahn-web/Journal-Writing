# Framing and confirmation

Users who are new to a paper type lose the thread when the framing is implicit. Every deliverable in this and later stages starts with one paragraph that says, in plain words:

1. The question the paper answers (for example, "what hourly carbon-free procurement costs a firm, with forecast accuracy as an input variable", not "a forecasting paper").
2. What the paper is not, especially if the user's own expertise could pull the reading the other way.
3. How it connects to the user's field or institutional brief.
4. An everyday analogy when the mechanism is new to the user.

Put the paper's object (cost, strategy, market) before the technique (forecasting, model) in the working title.

Confirmation: list each open decision with two or three options on short lines, mark the recommended one, and accept "all as recommended" as an answer. Typical decisions: load or demand assumption, model inputs, first and second target journal, base-case prices or penalties. Record the answers in memory the same day.
