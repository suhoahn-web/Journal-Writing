# Reading records and synthesis

## One record per paper

- Question and setting (country, market, decision-maker).
- Method and data, including the matching, settlement or evaluation rule when the topic depends on it.
- Key findings as numbers with units, copied from the text, tables or figures; say which.
- Limitations and future work in the authors' own terms; mark explicitly whether they name this study's gap.
- Relation to this study: same data, same method, same question, or a result the design must accommodate.
- Uncertainty about the record: truncated reading, figures read from an image, non-published version.

Records are what every later stage cites from. If a sentence in the manuscript cannot be traced to a record, it is rewritten or dropped.

## Synthesis

1. Evidence table by stream with one line per paper and the finding that matters for this study.
2. Gap count: how many authors state the gap as future work, and which papers are closest; a competing preprint is named, with what it lacks.
3. Anchor candidates: papers in the target journal whose data-to-results structure matches this study (see paper-writing).
4. Design revisions: each one a sentence of what changes, why, and which papers motivate it. In practice a review of about one hundred papers produced four revisions (a second decision rule as baseline, error characterised on several axes instead of one, two storage operating modes, and a price sensitivity with a break-even), and the first-round results were recomputed under the revised design.
5. Verify list: non-published versions quoted, journals pending indexing checks, primary sources (rules, notices, prices) still to be cited.
