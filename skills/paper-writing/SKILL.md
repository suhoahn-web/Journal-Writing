# Paper Writing

Write from completed evidence, not an invented story. Preserve the user's results, terminology, section structure, paragraph count, and approved wording unless the user authorizes a change.

Apply the repository's [working principles](../../PRINCIPLES.md) throughout.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the research materials, results, target journal, and literature before drafting.
2. Select key papers from the target journal where possible. Prioritize similarity of analytical method and data-to-results structure over superficial topic similarity, especially from Data onward. Additional literature must be verified SCI, SCIE, or SSCI journal articles.
3. Read key papers paragraph by paragraph. Report exactly one item per source paragraph. For the paragraph being followed, identify every sentence's function, content, and whether it has a citation; do not substitute a thematic summary.
4. Before each draft paragraph, report in this order: the key paper's sentence-level logic; what the user's paper must cover; the proposed sentence-level plan; the English draft and, when requested, a faithful Korean version.
5. Make each paragraph's first sentence state its central message. Keep lengths broadly comparable to the key paper; any user-set minimum sentence count is a hard constraint.
6. Verify every cited claim against the full paper. Save missing papers in the project's literature-review folder. If lawful access fails, immediately provide the full title for the user to obtain; if it remains unavailable, use a verified replacement rather than guessing.
7. Insert tables and figures where the argument first needs them and cite them in numerical order. Keep captions concise; put sample details, tests, symbols, and display conventions in notes, and interpretation in the body.
8. Assemble only approved paragraphs. Never repartition, merge, shorten, or regenerate completed sections on personal judgment.

## Boundary

Writing ends after the manuscript, references, tables, figures, captions, and submission statements are consistently assembled. Review is a separate minor-revision workflow; do not silently keep rewriting after review begins.
